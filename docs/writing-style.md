# Writing style guide

## Voice

- First person, conversational, present-tense narration of "here's what I did/found".
- Genuine enthusiasm without hype. Excitement is earned by the detail, not by adjectives.
- Warm self-deprecation about your own limits ("questionable soldering skills") rather
  than false modesty or apology.
- Curious rather than authoritative: pose the questions you don't have answers to
  instead of papering over gaps.
- Credit people and communities by name/link when they contributed an idea or fix.

## Sentence rhythm

- Mix short punchy declaratives with longer explanatory sentences. A one-word or
  one-line sentence ("Result!") can stand alone for emphasis after a longer build-up.
- Don't flatten everything to the same clause length — vary it deliberately, especially
  between narrative paragraphs and technical steps.

## Asides and parentheticals

- Use parenthetical asides liberally to add personality, a clarification, or a small
  joke without derailing the sentence, e.g. "(sorry for the giant image)".
- These should read as something you'd say to a friend mid-story, not a footnote.

## Blending technical detail with narrative

- Don't separate "the story" from "the how-to". Technical steps arrive inside the
  narrative of doing the thing, with the reasoning for each step explained as you go.
- It's fine for a post to be mostly technical — keep the narrator present throughout
  (what you tried, what surprised you, what you'd do differently) rather than switching
  into a detached reference-manual voice.
- State uncertainty plainly ("I'm unsure if this is because...") instead of asserting
  things you haven't verified.

## Structure

- Headings and lists are welcome for genuinely sequential or enumerable content, but
  don't over-fragment prose that reads better as paragraphs.
- Links go inline, pointing at sources, further reading, or prior related posts.

## Openings and closings

- Open by dropping straight into the scene, the problem, or the result — not a
  throat-clearing "In this post I will...".
- Close reflectively: what's still unresolved, what you're curious about next, or a
  modest sign-off. A short "further reading" section with links is a good way to end a
  post that leans on other people's work.

## What to avoid

- Marketing language, superlatives without backing, and hedge-free absolute claims.
- Long unbroken technical walls with no narrator voice.
- Over-apologising — one honest aside about a limitation is enough; don't repeat it.
